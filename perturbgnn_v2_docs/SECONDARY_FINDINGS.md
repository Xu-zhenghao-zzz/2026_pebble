# Secondary Findings — Beyond the Phgr1 Headline

This document collects findings from the v2 + v2.1 analysis that are
**statistically weaker than the Phgr1 headline but biologically or
methodologically meaningful**. They are not strong enough to be main
claims, but they:

1. Suggest follow-up biological hypotheses worth testing
2. Reveal cross-cutting patterns hidden by the Phgr1 narrative
3. Pre-empt research directions before competitors publish them

These should be reported in Supplementary §N.6 ("Secondary findings and
biological hypotheses") of the v2 paper, with the explicit caveat that
they are exploratory.

---

## N.6.1 — Effect heterogeneity across responses (AIPW τ̂ / Durbin δ ratio)

### Finding

The ratio of AIPW doubly robust estimate to v2's Durbin δ varies
dramatically across the five Phgr1 responses:

| Response | AIPW τ̂ | Durbin δ | Ratio τ̂/δ | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| macrophage | +0.29 | +0.35 | **0.82** | "Clean" causal effect, minimal confounding |
| endothelial | -0.06 | -0.10 | 0.60 | Moderate confounding |
| ifn_response | +0.14 | +0.27 | 0.52 | Moderate confounding |
| **fibroblast** | +0.15 | +0.62 | **0.24** | 76% of apparent effect is confounding |
| **hypoxia** | +0.39 | +1.34 | **0.29** | 71% of apparent effect is confounding |

### Why this matters

The q-value table (`cross_cohort_replicated.csv`) treats all five
responses as equivalent evidence for "Phgr1 is an NCA hub". But the AIPW
ratio reveals they are **not equivalent**:

- **macrophage response is the most causal** — most likely to survive
  a JAK-inhibitor rescue experiment
- **fibroblast response is mostly niche confounding** — Phgr1 source
  bins happen to sit in fibroblast-rich regions; the apparent δ is
  a marker of niche co-localisation, not a Phgr1 → fibroblast causal
  pathway

### Biological hypothesis

If we ever run a JAK-inhibitor rescue experiment (BIOLOGY_BACKLOG.md),
we predict:
- **macrophage response will be most strongly rescued** (highest τ̂/δ)
- **fibroblast response will be only partially rescued** (most of it
  is niche-driven, not JAK-STAT-driven)
- **hypoxia response will also be partially rescued** (hypoxia is
  known to interact with JAK-STAT but also with HIF-1α independently)

This is a **falsifiable prediction** generated by the AIPW analysis.

### Statistical caveat

The AIPW τ̂ estimates on M002 are tighter (n_source=2409) than M001
(n_source=1471), and on M003 they are uninformative (n_source=179 → all
CIs cross 0). The τ̂/δ ratios above are M001 values; M002 shows similar
patterns but with smaller magnitude differences across responses.

## N.6.2 — M003 late-stage attenuation hypothesis

### Finding

Across all four EGAT variants, the Phgr1 effect is preserved on M001
and M002 but **completely lost on M003**:

| Encoder | M001 δ | M002 δ | M003 δ | M003 p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 set-pooling | +0.62 | +0.48 | (not computed separately) | — |
| EGAT v1 | +0.10 | +0.40 | +0.05 | 0.124 |
| EGAT v3 | +0.11 | +0.40 | +0.007 | **0.915** |
| EGAT v4 | +0.08 | +0.38 | +0.011 | 0.746 |

### Why this matters

M003 is the largest cohort-1 slice (488 373 bins, vs M001 273 886 and
M002 358 149). It is also histologically the most advanced lung
metastasis (highest malignant fraction, lowest immune infiltration
based on cell-type composition).

The fact that **all EGAT variants fail on M003 but v2 set-pooling does
not** (Phgr1 × macrophage passes the cross-cohort gate including M003
in v2's original analysis) suggests:

- v2 set-pooling's tight information bottleneck extracts a signal
  that message-passing destroys on M003
- The signal may be **weaker** on M003 (effect attenuation in late-stage
  disease) such that any information loss pushes it below detection

### Biological hypothesis

The Phgr1 NCA effect may be **an early-tumour event**:
- Early-stage tumours (M001, M002): immune-fibroblast TME still
  organised, Phgr1's paracrine signals propagate normally
- Late-stage tumours (M003): malignant-cell-dominated TME, paracrine
  signals drowned out by tumour-autonomous signalling

If true, this would mean Phgr1 is a **prognostic marker for early-stage
disease** specifically, not for late-stage. The TCGA LUAD validation
(median OS Q1=20mo vs Q4=22.7mo, only 2.7-month difference) is
consistent with this — TCGA samples mixed stages, the early-stage
effect is diluted.

### Statistical caveat

M003 has only 179 Phgr1 source bins (vs M001 1471, M002 2409). The
signal loss may be partially due to small-sample AIPW/EGAT
instability rather than biology. Cannot disentangle without more
late-stage replicates.

## N.6.3 — NTC pool scarcity in M002 (0.05%)

### Finding

The NTC bin fraction varies dramatically across cohort-1 slices:

| Slice | Total bins | NTC bins | NTC fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| M001 | 273 886 | 548 | 0.20% |
| **M002** | **358 149** | **191** | **0.05%** |
| M003 | 488 373 | 1 813 | 0.37% |

M002 has only **1 NTC per 1875 bins** — four-fold scarcer than M001.

### Why this matters

v2 borrows M001+M003 NTCs for M002's vs-NTC analyses (M002's own 191
NTCs are too few for stable null estimation). This introduces a
**cross-slice baseline assumption** that is not explicitly tested.

The embedding-matched control framework (Layer 2) partially mitigates
this: matching is within-slice, so M002's 191 NTCs are matched 1:1
against M002 sources. But:
- M002 has 2 409 Phgr1 sources competing for 191 NTC controls
- Each NTC must be reused ~12× as a matched control
- This violates the independence assumption of permutation tests

### Methodological hypothesis

A reviewer could legitimately ask: "is your M002 Phgr1 δ real, or an
artefact of NTC pool exhaustion?" Our AIPW analysis (Fix 1) provides
partial defence — AIPW doesn't depend on NTC pool size — but only
on M001 (where NTC is adequate). M002's AIPW τ̂ is computed but with
the same NTC scarcity caveat.

### Actionable

Future SPAC-seq experiments should **spike in additional NTC guides**
to bring NTC fraction to ≥1% (5-20× current levels). This is a wet-lab
recommendation, not a v2 paper change.

## N.6.4 — Bcam mouse-human paradox

### Finding

Bcam appears in the cross-cohort replicated hits (combined q=4.4e-11,
malignant δ=+0.47) but shows an **opposite-direction signal in TCGA**:

```
SPAC-seq (mouse):
  Bcam × malignant module score: δ = +0.47 (Bcam source →↑malignant)
  Interpretation: Bcam perturbation makes neighbours more malignant-like

TCGA LUAD (human):
  BCAM high expression → Cox HR > 1 (worse prognosis)
  Interpretation: BCAM marks aggressive tumours
```

### Why this matters

At first glance these are concordant (both suggest Bcam → malignancy).
But the **mechanism is opposite**:

- **Mouse SPAC-seq reading**: Bcam perturbation (CRISPR knockdown)
  increases neighbours' malignant signature → Bcam is a **tumour
  suppressor** in this assay (loss → more malignant)
- **Human TCGA reading**: BCAM high expression → worse prognosis →
  BCAM is an **oncogene** marker

These cannot both be true mechanistically. Three possible resolutions:

1. **Bcam is not the causal gene**. The CRISPR guide has an off-target
   effect on a different gene that is the real tumour suppressor.
   Cohort 2 has 2 guides per gene; we should check if both guides
   reproduce the malignancy effect.

2. **Bcam has context-dependent roles**. In mouse MC38 lung metastasis
   (acute perturbation), Bcam loss drives de-differentiation. In
   human LUAD (chronic adaptation), BCAM expression marks an
   aggressive state that arose through different selection pressure.

3. **The TCGA signal is cell-type-confounded**. BCAM is expressed in
   endothelial cells. LUAD samples with high BCAM may have more
   endothelial content (more vascular tumours), which independently
   predicts worse prognosis. The "BCAM → malignancy" reading is then
   a marker of vascular density, not gene function.

### Statistical caveat

SUBMISSION_CHECKLIST.md already flagged Bcam as a "counter-example"
(gene with large δ but opposite Cox HR), used to demonstrate v2
doesn't just chase effect size. But the paradox deserves deeper
investigation. Cohort 2 has 2 guides for Bcam — if both reproduce
the malignancy effect, off-target becomes less likely (resolution 1).

### Actionable

This is a candidate for a follow-up paper. The Bcam paradox is exactly
the kind of biology that spatial CRISPR + TCGA cross-validation is
designed to surface.

## N.6.5 — Rab8a as an under-explored NCA hub

### Finding

Rab8a × malignant is the second-strongest cross-cohort hit, almost as
significant as Phgr1's headline:

| Hit | Response | n_slices | combined_q | mean δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phgr1 | fibroblast | 7 | 3.0e-60 | +0.43 |
| **Rab8a** | **malignant** | **6** | **5.0e-56** | **+0.49** |
| Phgr1 | macrophage | 7 | 1.1e-41 | +0.41 |
| Phgr1 | hypoxia | 7 | 6.1e-21 | +0.44 |

Rab8a's q (5e-56) is within 4 orders of magnitude of Phgr1 fibroblast
(3e-60), and its effect size (+0.49) is larger.

### Why this matters

Rab8a is barely mentioned in the v2 paper. The narrative is dominated
by Phgr1. But:

- **Rab8a's effect size (+0.49) is the largest in the entire scan**
  (larger than any Phgr1 response)
- **Rab8a is a single-response hub** (only malignant), but a stronger
  one than Phgr1's macrophage/hypoxia
- **Rab8a has known membrane trafficking biology** (Rab GTPase family)
  → mechanistically interpretable as paracrine vesicle release

### Why Rab8a is not the headline

Three reasons:
1. **Phgr1 has 5 replicated responses, Rab8a has 1** — Phgr1 is a
   multi-response hub, more central to the TME
2. **Phgr1 has TCGA survival closure** (Cox HR=0.556), Rab8a does not
   (we have not checked TCGA for RAB8A)
3. **Phgr1 is "novel"** (function unknown), Rab8a is "known" (Rab
   GTPase) — novelty bias

### Biological hypothesis

Rab8a's restriction to the malignant response suggests a different
mechanism: **Rab8a source bins alter neighbours' malignant phenotype
via vesicle-mediated signalling**. This could be exosomes, ectosomes,
or direct cell-cell contact (Rab8a is involved in polarised secretion).

If Phgr1 is the "TME-state marker" hub, Rab8a might be the
"malignancy-modulating" hub. These are complementary biological roles.

### Actionable

v2 paper Discussion should explicitly mention Rab8a as a strong
secondary finding. The Discussion currently focuses on Phgr1, with
Rab8a only in the cross-cohort table. **Adding 1-2 paragraphs on
Rab8a** would:
- Show we are not over-focusing on Phgr1
- Open up the membrane-trafficking angle as a parallel mechanism
- Pre-empt a competitor publishing "Rab8a is the real hub" before us

## N.6.6 — Phgr1 ICB direction instability across cancer types

### Finding

The "Phgr1 does not predict ICB response" finding (v2 §11, F18) hides
a more nuanced pattern. The HRs across 4 cohorts:

| Cohort | Cancer type | HR | p | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IMvigor210 | Bladder | 0.87 | 0.34 | Protective |
| Liu melanoma | Melanoma | 0.92 | 0.58 | Protective |
| **Hellmann lung** | **Lung** | **1.12** | **0.67** | **Harmful** |
| Bladder alt | Bladder | 0.95 | 0.71 | Neutral |

3 of 4 cohorts point in the **protective direction** (consistent with
TCGA LUAD Cox HR=0.556). But the lung ICB cohort points in the
**opposite direction**.

### Why this matters

The current v2 paper says "Phgr1 does not predict ICB response" — this
is technically true (no individual cohort reaches p<0.05) but
**scientifically misleading**. The pattern is "consistent protective
direction in 3/4 cohorts, opposite in lung".

### Biological hypothesis

Phgr1's role in ICB response may be **cancer-type-specific**:

- In bladder + melanoma (lymphocyte-inflamed TMEs): Phgr1 marks the
  inflamed phenotype → high Phgr1 patients respond better to ICB
  (direction same as TCGA prognosis)
- In lung cancer (more heterogeneous TME, often "cold"): Phgr1 marks
  a different state that does not benefit from ICB, or is harmful
  in the context of anti-PD-1

### Statistical caveat

All 4 cohorts are underpowered (n=100-300), so the "direction
instability" could be noise. A meta-analysis with random effects would
give a more honest summary than the per-cohort view.

### Actionable

v2 paper §11 should be revised from "Phgr1 does NOT predict ICB" to
"Phgr1 ICB prediction is cancer-type-dependent, with consistent
protective direction in 3/4 cohorts but opposite direction in lung".
This is more honest and more interesting.

## Cross-cutting pattern: the "Phgr1 narrative" hides interesting biology

Looking at N.6.1 through N.6.6 together, a meta-pattern emerges:

**The v2 paper's narrative ("Phgr1 is the cross-cohort NCA hub")
compresses multiple distinct biological signals into a single headline.**

- N.6.1: Phgr1's 5 responses are not equivalent (macrophage is causal,
  fibroblast is confounded)
- N.6.2: Phgr1 may be an early-stage event, not a late-stage one
- N.6.3: Phgr1's M002 result has hidden NTC-scarcity caveat
- N.6.4: Bcam's paradox is suppressed by the Phgr1 focus
- N.6.5: Rab8a is a parallel hub, under-explored
- N.6.6: Phgr1's ICB story is more nuanced than "negative"

None of these challenge the core claim. But they reveal that **the
current paper's framing optimises for one clean story at the cost of
multiple interesting subplots**.

### Recommendation for the paper revision

Add Supplementary §N.6 with these six subsections. Frame them as:

> *"Beyond the Phgr1 cross-cohort replication headline, our analysis
> surfaced six secondary findings that are statistically weaker but
> biologically suggestive. We report them here as exploratory
> observations and as directions for follow-up work, not as
> established claims."*

This costs the paper ~3-4 pages of supplementary material and gains:
- Reviewer credit for honesty and depth
- Pre-emption of competitor publications on Rab8a / Bcam / cancer-type
  specificity
- A richer discussion section that engages with biology rather than
  just statistics
